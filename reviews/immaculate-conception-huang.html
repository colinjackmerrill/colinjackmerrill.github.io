Immaculate Conception

Ling Ling Huang

★★★★☆ Read November 9, 2025

If you like literary-speculative fiction that blurs art, obsession, and technology, this one’s worth checking out. It follows Enka, a struggling artist who becomes entangled with the dazzling Mathilde, and explores the possibilities of a new technology called the Scaffold that links minds in the name of empathy and creative collaboration. The result is part art-world fever dream, part psychological thriller, paced like a slow burn that builds to something raw and unnerving.

This book teetered right on the edge of me really liking it. While the razor-sharp writing and blistering emotions had me devouring the novel in only a few sittings, the Scaffold concept and the way Enka connects her mind with Mathilde felt contrived, a little too gimmicky. Still, Huang’s prose and emotional insight nearly overcame that. The psychology at play was fascinating, and I enjoyed seeing how easily admiration blurs into fixation, and good intentions curdle into something murkier. I loved watching their friendship evolve from awe to dependency to quiet destruction. The art scenes and Enka’s hunger to be great grounded the book in something painfully human.

What stuck with me most was the tension between devotion and desire, and how ambition can corrode empathy. The mind-link premise sometimes veered into cringe territory, but Huang always reeled it back with beautiful, devastating clarity. I was frequently caught between fascination and discomfort, in a wonderful way.

I rate Immaculate Conception a 3.75/5 because it’s a fearless, unnerving exploration of art, envy, and identity, but it didn’t win me over completely.

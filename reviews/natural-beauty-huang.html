Natural Beauty

Ling Ling Huang

★★★☆☆ Read January 22, 2026

Ling Ling Huang’s Natural Beauty is an ambitious, unsettling debut that blends literary fiction, speculative horror, and social critique.

The novel follows a young pianist whose life unravels after her parents’ health collapses, pushing her into an elite beauty and wellness world where perfection comes at increasingly disturbing costs.

While the plot itself is simple, the experience is not. Huang excels at atmosphere, delivering moments of visceral body horror that genuinely unsettled me (in a good way, mostly). Many scenes were effectively surprising because they juxtaposed the intimate with the grotesque.

Where the book works best is its exploration of the protagonist’s complicated relationship with her parents, shaped by love, obligation, resentment, and grief. The novel also raises compelling questions about bodily autonomy, perfectionism, assimilation, and identity, especially within the context of beauty culture and experimental science.

Still, the execution is uneven. The tone wobbles between horror, satire, and melodrama, and at times the emotional beats feel overplayed rather than earned. A few moments pulled me out of the story entirely. While I understood what the novel was reaching for, the protagonist’s behavior sometimes stretched credibility, making it difficult to stay fully invested in the emotional stakes.

Overall, I wish Huang had been able to better control and unify the many compelling ideas in a way that fully landed for me.

That said, if you’re in the mood for something uncomfortable, idea-driven, and a little messy (and you enjoy books like Severance or My Year of Rest and Relaxation) this may work for you.

I didn’t love Natural Beauty as much as Huang’s Immaculate Conception (start there!) but both works makes it clear she’s a writer worth watching, and I can’t wait to read whatever she writes next!

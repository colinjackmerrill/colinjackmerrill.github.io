Book Reviews

The Elements

John Boyne

★★★★☆ Read November 22, 2025

4.25/5 Stars

John Boyne’s The Elements is an ambitious exploration of guilt, trauma, complicity, and the possibility of redemption. Boyne links four novellas in which he follows a woman fleeing scandal, a young athlete on trial, a surgeon shaped by childhood wounds, and a father traveling with his son.

He writes with a controlled intensity that lets the emotional weight land without melodrama. The characters are deeply flawed yet rendered with empathy, and their private shame and fear often feel painfully close. One line captures the book’s raw honesty about the damage we carry:

“I used to think the past was a place I could outrun, but it waits for me everywhere I go.”

The connections among the four narratives are give the book a cumulative force. Still, the repetition of trauma and the increasingly tight coincidences can feel numbing by the final section.

This is a heavy read that does not look away from the realities of abuse, guilt, and the long fallout of silence, so emotional readiness is essential. Readers who appreciate the intensity of novels like A Little Life will find similar power here.

Overall, this is a challenging and gripping novel: unsettling, absorbing, and morally complex. Even in its darkest moments, The Elements offers small but meaningful glimpses of humanity.

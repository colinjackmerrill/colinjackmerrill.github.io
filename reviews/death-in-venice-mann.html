Death in Venice

Thomas Mann

★★★☆☆ Read February 1, 2026

Have you ever finished a book feeling certain it was important without being entirely sure you enjoyed it? That was Death in Venice for me.

Thomas Mann's 1912 classic follows Gustav, an aging German writer who travels to Venice and becomes consumed by an obsession with a beautiful Polish boy he never speaks to. That's the whole plot. What Mann does with it is layer symbolism, Greek philosophy, and psychological dread, all while an epidemic quietly closes in around the edges.

The prose is meticulous, ornate, and rewards close reading, but it can also feel suffocating. Everything means something. Every stranger the protagonist encounters is a mythological signpost. It's brilliantly crafted, but its density keeps you at arm's length.

I respect this more than I was moved by it. For me, it's a masterwork of atmosphere and ideas that left me admiring Mann's control while wishing he'd let me feel a little more and think a little less.

3.25/5 Stars.

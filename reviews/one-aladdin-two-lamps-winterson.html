One Aladdin Two Lamps

Jeanette Winterson

★★★★☆ Read February 1, 2026

Jeanette Winterson doesn't write books so much as she builds kaleidoscopes and dares you to look through them.

One Aladdin Two Lamps takes One Thousand and One Nights as its launchpad and then spirals outward into memoir, literary criticism, philosophy, and political commentary, all held together by Winterson's fierce argument that storytelling is how we survive. She retells Shahrazad's tales, threads in her own upbringing as a queer girl in a fundamentalist household, name-drops everyone from Shakespeare to Taylor Swift, and somehow makes it all feel like a single conversation rather than a syllabus.

When it works, it's thrilling. Winterson's prose has a clarity and directness that cuts through the sheer density of what she's attempting. But the book occasionally buckles under its own ambition. Some passages tip from passionate into preachy, and the late-stage detour into AI optimism feels oddly disconnected from the rest.

3.5/5 Stars. A dazzling, uneven love letter to the power of stories.

The Art Thief: A True Story of Love, Crime, and a Dangerous Obsession

Michael Finkel

★★★★☆ Read December 10, 2025

“He takes only works that stir him emotionally, and seldom the most valuable piece in a place. He feels no remorse when he steals because museums, in his deviant view, are really just prisons for art.”

In The Art Thief, I was fascinated to learn about bibliomania (an obsessive, sometimes compulsive craving to collect books or art objects) and about Stendhal syndrome, a real condition where people can experience dizziness, anxiety, or even fainting when confronted with overwhelming beauty. These ideas sit at the center of the story of Stéphane Breitwieser, who stole 239 artworks from 172 European museums between 1995 and 2001… not for profit, but out of pure, consuming admiration. At one point, he even describes his obsession with devastating simplicity: “Art is my drug.” (I need this on a t-shirt!)

Finkel’s prose is clean, fast, and almost novelistic (though with very little dialogue), which makes this wild spree incredibly readable. The tone stays curious rather than sensational, allowing Breitwieser’s audacity and his strange, intense relationship to beauty to carry the book.

It’s no surprise this was a Goodreads Choice Award Nominee for Readers’ Favorite Nonfiction (2023). The blend of true crime, psychology, and art history makes it both accessible and compelling, even for someone like me who isn’t a heavy nonfiction reader.

The Art Thief is engaging, fascinating, easy to recommend, and absolutely worth the read.

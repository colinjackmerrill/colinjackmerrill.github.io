Chess Story

Stefan Zweig

★★★★☆ Read October 14, 2025

Chess Story is a concise, psychologically sharp novella about obsession, memory, and the strange hold a game can have over the mind. On a ship to Buenos Aires, a world-champion chess player crosses paths with an unlikely opponent, and what begins as a simple match pulls into deeper territory. Chess becomes more than a game, and thinking turns inward in unsettling ways.

Zweig writes with precision and restraint; every page feels purposeful. Even as someone who already loves chess, I appreciated how the book captures both the thrill of the game and the claustrophobia of over-thinking it.

The ending is abrupt and left me wanting a bit more, but it feels in keeping with the book’s focus on psychological tension over tidy resolution.

Chess Story is a short, thoughtful, and quietly gripping read that sits with you after you finish it.

Paradise Logic

Sophie Kemp

★★★☆☆ Read February 28, 2026

Paradise Logic is 8% talking snakes, 13% excerpts from a fictional magazine called Girlfriend Weekly, 19% hallucinatory clinical trials, 15% sex scenes that will make you put the book down and stare at the ceiling, and 100% prose that reads like it was written by someone channeling Miranda July after three energy drinks and a Tumblr binge.

My reading experience felt like being trapped inside someone's unhinged group chat while they're having an existential crisis, and somehow being unable to stop scrolling.

The story follows Reality Kahn, a 23-year-old Oberlin grad living in Gowanus, Brooklyn, who goes on a surreal, fragmented, and frequently obscene odyssey to fulfill her life's purpose: finding a boyfriend and becoming the greatest girlfriend of all time.

Kemp's voice is the whole show here. Reality narrates her world with a mix of alien detachment and devastating sincerity, describing everything from sex to Coca-Cola with the same wide-eyed, off-kilter cadence. It's equally hilarious and cringey.

But make no mistake, Paradise Logic is a lot. A LOT. It knows it's a lot. Kemp knows exactly what she's doing tonally, switching between absurdist comedy and genuine heartbreak without warning, and the whiplash is part of the point. Underneath all the chaos, she sustains a sharp, sad argument about what it means to desire men in a world that punishes you for it.

The electricity of that voice will keep most readers going, at least those who aren't immediately put off. Still, I found that the narrative underneath it struggles to hold its own weight. The surreal detours pile up, the bit starts to wear thin in the back half, and by the end I wasn't sure the novel had earned its emotional crescendo so much as arrived at one. Whether the book works for you will depend entirely on how long you can ride a vibe before you start wanting a story. For me, it was about three-quarters of the way.

That said, I'm genuinely glad this book exists. It's doing something fearless, and any comparisons to Miranda July or Mona Awad feel right without fully capturing what Paradise Logic offers. I left wanting more from the bones of this one even as I admired it. Read it if you're in the mood to feel unsettled and entertained in equal measure. Skip it if you need narrative to be the thing pulling you forward.

3/5 Stars.

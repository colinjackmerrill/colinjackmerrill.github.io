The Loneliness of Sonia and Sunny

Kiran Desai

★★★☆☆ Read January 16, 2026

The Loneliness of Sonia and Sunny follows two Indian American siblings navigating questions of belonging and identity in the U.S. and later in India. The novel explores what it means to feel culturally split and emotionally unmoored, no matter which country you are in.

I liked this more than I disliked it, but I also struggled with it. The book felt long, but I understand why Desai gave it so much space. She’s attempting a broad portrait of what it means to grow up between cultures, shaped by migration, class, money, art, and family obligation, but that ambition often comes at the expense of pacing.

The novel frequently shifts focus to side characters. While these sections are thematically relevant, I consciously felt pulled away from the main characters. I can see how this fragmentation reflects the experience of growing up between cultures, but the emotional payoff didn’t always land for me.

Where the book worked best was in its quieter, more intimate moments, especially the relationship between Sonia and the artist, which captured tensions around creativity, desire, and self-definition.

This is an ambitious, contemplative work that sits alongside a lot of recent literary fiction that’s interested in questions of belonging and what it means to build a life across cultures. Ultimately, I admired what it was trying to do more than I enjoyed reading it.

3.25/5 ⭐️

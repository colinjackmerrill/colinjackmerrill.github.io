The Idiot

Elif Batuman

★★☆☆☆ Read February 24, 2026

Is the rock on the cover meant to suggest, “this is your brain on The Idiot”? Because I think I felt my brain slowly petrify while I read this book.

The Idiot follows Selin as she drifts through her first year of college. She befriends a small circle of eccentric classmates, enrolls in a Russian class that quietly shapes her inner life, and becomes fixated on a strange, elliptical email correspondence with an older Hungarian math student. Not much “happens” in a traditional plot sense, but the novel traces Selin’s attempts to interpret language, relationships, and herself.

I wanted to love this book. On paper, the awkward intellectual coming-of-age and deadpan humor should have worked. Yet, the narrators inability to turn experience into meaning became my own.

To her credit, Batuman fully commits to the bit. The voice is precise, alien, often very funny. The academic satire and early-email awkwardness are sharp and painfully accurate.

But admiration isn’t the same as enjoyment. The Ivan storyline circles without deepening, and the book’s refusal of catharsis started to feel airless rather than profound.

This is for readers who love interiority over plot, anti-climax over transformation, and the slow study of a mind thinking itself into corners. It just wasn’t for me.

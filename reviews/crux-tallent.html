Book Reviews

Crux

Gabriel Tallent

★★★★★ Read January 10, 2026

Thank you to Penguin Random House / Riverhead Books and NetGalley for the opportunity to read the advance reader copy of Crux in exchange for an honest review. All opinions are my own.

“I wanted a guarantee. But that’s not how it works. You find out by trying.”

That line captures the emotional heart of Crux, a novel that is as funny and tender as it is painfully honest.

Crux follows two longtime friends standing at a crossroads, trying to decide what kind of lives they are willing to live and what compromises they can survive.

What immediately stood out to me was the humor. Tallent's understanding of how people are funny with each other shines, and the banter on the page feels natural. The comedy becomes part of how the characters survive fear and uncertainty, and it never undercuts the seriousness of what the novel is doing.

Beneath that humor is an examination of meaning, ambition, and disillusionment. The book captures the dread of wasting your life, of grinding toward something you no longer believe in, and of realizing the world may not offer what you felt it promised.

Tallent renders the friendship at the center of Crux with emotional precision. I believed in it completely, and I cared deeply about both characters. Their interior lives are portrayed with sincerity. Their thoughts spiral, contradict themselves, and loop back in ways that feel true to how real minds work. Through this, the novel reveals how closely the desire to be exceptional is tied to the desire to be loved.

The book is steeped in climbing terminology, but the most important term is the titular one. A crux is the most difficult moment in a climb. The climbing metaphor that runs through the novel is especially effective, particularly when Tallent writes, “It’s cruxes that matter. The top is only a symbol.” The hardest moment, not the outcome, is what defines you.

Even as it wrestles with fear and uncertainty, the book feels funny, emotionally generous, and quietly hopeful, the kind of novel that leaves you reflective but satisfied.

Ultimately, Crux is about choosing to step into uncertainty without guarantees. It is about trying anyway and about making meaning rather than waiting for it to appear. That is what made this a five-star read for me. It felt true to friendship, to fear, to ambition, and to the terrifying freedom of realizing that no one is going to hand you meaning. You have to step into it yourself.

Recommended for readers who love character-driven literary fiction, sharp dialogue, and books that ask hard questions without offering easy answers.

Note: This novel contains themes of grief, child abuse, mental health struggles, substance use, and existential despair, all handled thoughtfully and with care.

Writing Tools & Resources

Tools

Interactive utilities for novelists and storytellers.

  • Novel Planner

    A workspace for mapping the moving parts of a novel in progress — characters, structure, midpoint, and more.

  • Character Psychology

    A generator for wound and false belief — the two things that exist before everything else in a character's life.

  • Scene Planner Coming Soon

    A workspace for the individual unit of story — purpose, conflict, POV, and what changes by the final line.

  • Premise Stress-Tester Coming Soon

    Put in a premise and interrogate it. Does it create irresolvable conflict? Does the want oppose the need? Find out before you write eighty pages.

  • Story Anatomy Coming Soon

    Describe your story and map it against the full framework — want, need, fear, false belief, mirror, opposing force, pivot, theme. An interactive extension of the Analyzing Narrative series.

  • First Line Workshop Coming Soon

    Write opening lines and examine what they promise, what they withhold, and whether they create the forward motion a first line needs.

  • Theme Extractor Coming Soon

    Describe your story and work backward to the theme underneath. Useful for writers who are deep in a draft and need to understand what they're actually writing about.

Resources

Reference guides and frameworks for understanding how fiction works.

  • The Twenty Master Plots

    The fundamental narrative situations underlying all fiction — from Quest and Pursuit to Transformation and Descension.

  • The Archetypal Characters

    The fundamental roles that recur across all storytelling — Hero, Shadow, Mentor, Trickster, and the rest.

  • Wounds & False Beliefs

    The psychological core beneath every compelling character — a glossary of formative wounds and the mistaken convictions they generate.

  • The Seven Basic Conflicts

    The fundamental types of opposition that drive every story — from Man vs. Self to Man vs. Society.

  • Common Narrative Structures

    A comparison of the major structural frameworks for fiction — the Three-Act Structure, Hero's Journey, Fichtean Curve, and more.

  • The Scene Checklist

    What every scene needs to do — and the questions to ask when one isn't working.

  • Craft Terms Glossary

    Plain-language definitions for the terms that come up most in writing about fiction — inciting incident, free indirect discourse, dramatic irony, and more.
